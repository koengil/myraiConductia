Source: design spec §11; decision-log D4

## The anti-Rogo story

The go-to-market narrative runs through a single contrast: Conductia is not Rogo.

Rogo answers "I want to buy Apple" with a data dump. Conductia answers
"I'm exploring a sale of Atlassian" by surfacing only the key decisions an MD
must make — with compliance omnipresent above everything.

This contrast drives every screen, every piece of sales copy, and every demo
beat. It is not an attack on Rogo's quality; it is a precise framing of a
different job. Rogo serves analysts producing output. Conductia serves MDs
making decisions under regulatory scrutiny.

For the full competitive breakdown — Rogo's per-analyst pricing misalignment,
its absence of compliance infrastructure, the SLM incumbent, the Rogo contrast
table — see [`../00-vision/strategy-and-moat.md`](../00-vision/strategy-and-moat.md).
Do not restate that argument here.

Source: design spec §2; `00-vision/strategy-and-moat.md`

## Compliance as the moat

Compliance is not a feature Conductia added on top. It is the architectural
choice that defines Conductia's competitive position. Every skill run, every
message, every output routes through the compliance layer before it reaches
the user or any external party.

The business case: investment banks face multi-billion-dollar regulatory fines
for non-compliant communications. No current AI player for financial services
has built compliance infrastructure into the core.

The moat argument is made in full at
[`../00-vision/strategy-and-moat.md § 1`](../00-vision/strategy-and-moat.md).
The growth narrative deploys it as a single, decisive claim in the selling
context — not an explanation, a credential.

Source: design spec §2; `00-vision/strategy-and-moat.md`

## MD-grade, sell-side

The primary audience is managing directors on the sell-side: senior, deal-driven,
under compliance scrutiny, uninterested in junior-analyst workflows.

The secondary audience for the current moment is **Storm** — the founder,
an ex-investment-banker — who will evaluate the platform against his own
experience of what an MD actually needs. The demo is a sales artifact aimed
at him specifically.

For full persona detail and jobs-to-be-done, see

## The three headline claims

When compressing the narrative to a sales moment or slide, these are the three
claims in order:

1. **Decisions, not data dumps.** Five ranked acquirers with scores and a
   one-line strategic rationale. Not a spreadsheet.
2. **Compliance omnipresent.** Every action monitored and checked before it
   executes. Former FINRA examiners on the advisory board.
3. **MD-native.** The platform is built for the person who makes the call,
   not the person who prepares the briefing.

Source: design spec §2, §5
