## Product vision & strategy

- **One-line identity:** the Conductia Deal Command Center is "the AI-native operating layer for live sell-side M&A execution": it ingests the deal's communications, documents, calendar, CRM, and trackers; maintains the authoritative deal plan; routes work to the right human, agent, or pair; catches errors before anything leaves the firm; and preserves a complete, supervised record of what happened, why, and who approved it (Blueprint §3).
- **What it is NOT** (repeated across every document): "not a chatbot. And not an analyst-replacement tool. It does not sit and wait for prompts, and it does not exist to produce one more draft"; final form: "It is not a chatbot. It is not an analyst tool." The 2-pager adds "it does not replace the banker" — the banker keeps the judgment, the decisions, and the relationship (Blueprint §3 + §18; Vision2p 'WHAT IT IS').
- **Final definition / land-and-expand:** "Conductia is the governed operating layer for how investment banking work gets done… Banks meet it as the Deal Command Center for a live sell-side process. They keep it as the operating backbone for the firm." It handles routing, policy, supervision, recordkeeping, and human-gated approvals across people and AI agents, and is the persistent system of record for the firm's institutional memory (Blueprint §18; Founders §6.1 slide 18).
- **Mission:** "Make high-value professional services as scalable as a software business, starting with investment banking" — productize the delivery of regulated advisory work: capture institutional knowledge, route tasks across humans and AI agents, reduce senior-banker rework, increase proactive client engagement, prevent dropped balls, embed compliance controls directly into the workflow (Blueprint MISSION).
- **Scope narrowness is a feature:** exactly one workflow — sell-side M&A, advising the owner of a company being sold and running the entire sale. "Not buy-side. Not IPOs. Not trading. One workflow: a live mandate" (Handbook §1 'The domain').
- **North Star + wedge:** "Conductia becomes the AI-native operating system for regulated investment banking workflows"; the first wedge is the deliberately narrow, urgent Sell-Side Deal Command Center — manage the workflow spine of a live process before automating every work product (Blueprint §7).
- **The five magical experiences** (adoption earned day one, one per audience): (1) morning command queue for the banker; (2) inbox-to-operating-plan for the deal record; (3) background economics — cost/quality optimization and spend visibility — for the CFO/COO; (4) supervisory window (live view, not after-the-fact reconstruction) for compliance; (5) the compounding firm — institutional memory reuse without leaking client data or training models on it — for the managing partner (Blueprint §4).
- **Target market / GTM:** sell-side M&A at boutiques and mid-market banks "where the pain is acute, the decision-makers are reachable, and procurement is lighter than a bulge-bracket"; Storm's network is the unfair advantage; motion is design partners → paid pilots → platform, run by forward-deployed ex-bankers (Founders §8.1; Deck slide 14).
- **Economic thesis:** "Banking doesn't scale" — revenue scales only by adding people, and people consume roughly two-thirds of every dollar (Perella ~67% comp/revenue, Moelis ~67%, PJT ~69%, Houlihan ~63%, Lazard ~66%, Evercore ~66%; IB advisory gross margin ~40–55% vs ~75%+ software). Of ~$137.6B 2025 global IB fees, ~66%/$91B is compensation. "The value is Judgment, but the costs are in process" — process is the bulk of hours, a fraction of value; every revenue-originating MD carries 1–2x junior/execution headcount. Insight: "Automate the process. Sell the judgment." (Deck slides 2–4, 17; Vision2p 'WHAT IT IS').
- **Senior-leverage positioning:** "You do not replace the MD; you make the firm's most valuable people far more scalable." Competitive framing: "Tools make a banker faster. Conductia runs the deal. Rogo solves junior productivity; we solve senior productivity, which is worth far more" (Founders §6.1 slide 4; Deck slide 9).
- **Compliance-first stance:** "Compliance is not a section of this product. It is the reason the product can exist inside a bank" (§6 is titled 'Compliance Is the Unlock (but not the product)'). AI becomes usable in regulated banking only when every action is permissioned, supervised, logged, and human-gated — Conductia makes that the default. Wedge sentence: "Competitors selling a chatbot ask a bank to trust an unsupervised model. Conductia asks a bank to supervise AI the way it already supervises people" (Blueprint §6; Deck slide 8; Founders §6.1 slide 8).
- **Proactive-compliance reframe (latest):** Vision2p flips compliance from reactive to proactive — today violations are caught after the fact; here AI screens every output (human and agent) before it leaves, agents cross-check agents, every action lands in a searchable auditable record. JPMorgan-scale banks pay hundreds of millions per year in FINRA/SEC fines despite more compliance staff than bankers — "prevention is the product. Positioned to help compliance, never compete with it" (Vision2p 'Why a bank lets it in the door').
- **The one invariant** (everything hangs off it): "agents draft, retrieve & propose — humans and deterministic code decide, gate, approve & release." Anything that grants access, enforces an information wall, approves a release, or picks the authoritative number is testable code or a named human — never an LLM, "because a regulator will not accept 'the model decided' as a control." Storm calls this "make or break"; agents check "even the stupid stuff." Declared cross-role and non-negotiable in the engineering handbook: disagreements about "can an agent do X?" resolve against the invariant; disagreements about a word resolve against the glossary (Handbook §0 + §0b; Vision2p 'The one rule'; RTS-notes transcript).
- **Three permanent constraints** the whole architecture derives from: (1) Secrecy — MNPI/insider-trading risk → code names, information barriers, deal-linked ingestion; (2) Supervision — an unsupervised tool is a FINRA 3110 violation → human gates every external action, agents capped + killable; (3) Reconstructability — a regulator can demand the complete record years later → everything emits an append-only AuditEvent (Handbook §0 'The one idea').
- **Why now — two gates opened together:** the quality gate (frontier models finally good enough to draft, check, and reason over real deal work) and the regulatory gate (FINRA Notice 24-09, June 2024, + the 2026 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report, Dec 2025, give first-time guidance on supervised AI agents — permissioned, logged, human-gated AI is now usable inside a bank). "The window to own the category is open and narrow" (Deck slide 7; Founders §6.1 slide 3; Blueprint §14 + Appendix B).
- **Twelve core principles** (Blueprint §8): workflow first / AI second (AI operates inside the process, not a blank chatbot); owner + execution mode (Human/Agent/Both) on every task; compliance by architecture, not bolted on; permission-aware everything; reversible actions automatable with logs vs irreversible requiring human approval; model-agnostic and sovereignty-ready (no single model vendor holds leverage); labor value as the unit of value (price against value, not tokens); code names by default; firm-configurable governance; expert panels as a quality layer (real published frameworks only, never fabricated quotes; review layer not core dependency in v1); persistent institutional memory with stateless models (models are swappable engines; the firm's compounding intelligence is the asset and the firm owns it); data rights are strategic (Blueprint §8).
- **The selection test — the brand-protecting line:** an application ships at launch only if it is both highly valuable AND executable flawlessly — "flawless execution is the gate, because trust is the whole product in a regulated setting." Ships: bounded, sourced, human-gated work (mechanical DCF on management's projections, comps from sourced data, drafts a human approves). Waits: open-ended generative authoring of judgment-heavy deliverables. Worked distinction: "Conductia computes the DCF, the comps, and the precedents… and the banker owns the valuation advocacy. Compute the analysis; the human owns the argument" (Founders §4.2).
- **Deferred applications + strategic rationale:** explicitly deferred — financial model building (final numbers must come from a deterministic model of record, not an LLM), CIM authoring, management-presentation drafting, valuation advocacy, negotiation copilot. Storm's rationale: everyone else started with the most complex, judgment-heavy work (models, decks) — "insanely stupid" — whereas starting with everything else means Conductia is "not getting displaced by the model"; point tools building models/decks "will eventually get subsumed by the model" (Founders §4.5; RTS-notes transcript; Vision2p 'HOW IT'S USED').
- **Platform model — "the Apple one":** a governed platform with applications on top that must run natively and follow its rules. Conductia owns and controls the platform; applications come from Conductia or third parties under revenue-share. Third-party apps run only inside the Command Center's runtime controls (sandboxing, permissioning, governed retrieval, audit logging) and supply logic and prompts, never direct access to firm data — the bank's compliance perimeter is never breached by external code (Blueprint §7 'The platform model').
- **Platform-not-app moat argument** (three legs): (1) standalone apps can't win — point tools are thin wrappers the model will eventually swallow, and banks/regulators won't trust a wrapper with a live deal; (2) compliance is the perimeter — supervision, permissions, audit, and information barriers must live in the platform; apps comply or don't ship; (3) own the workflows, not the model — the governed workflow graph, permissioned process data, and institutional memory are what a wrapper can never replicate. "Models are swappable; the layer a bank trusts is not" (Deck slide 8; Founders §6.1 slide 12).
- **Where the moat lives (all sources agree):** the deterministic governance core / governed workflow graph, permissioned process data, per-token cost attribution, compliance layer, institutional memory — never the models. Highest-moat components (Pre-Send QC screen, permissions/barriers/code-name service, approval/gate engine, orchestrator) must be owned and never outsourced (Handbook §5; Blueprint §17; Founders §3.2, §4.1; Deck slide 8).
- **Competitive quadrant:** "Everyone is racing to the same empty corner." Rogo, Hebbia, Blueflame, OffDeal, Mosaic, Crunched, Keye, Harvey, Maywood, Model ML, Bloomberg/FactSet, DealCloud, Copilot plotted on Point-tool↔Full-platform × General/horizontal↔Compliance-grade/deal-native axes — Conductia alone occupies full-platform + compliance-grade, deal-native (Deck slide 9).
- **Rogo evidence:** "Ignatius trialed Rogo for three weeks and walked away — output tools without rails don't stick." If model-displaced point tools succeed, "great — you're number seventh in our process, put your application on top" (Storm's verbatim framing at the RTS kickoff) (Vision2p 'WHY IT WINS'; RTS-notes transcript).
- **What Rogo is shipping:** Rogo's own Enhanced Projects one-pager shows a document-Q&A workspace — upload hundreds of files / an entire VDR into a project, built-in viewer (incl. password-protected files), cited answers across the file tree, cross-project search of precedents and past deliverables, shareable/forkable project chats — and its closing line publicly roadmaps Conductia's territory: "Expect continued improvements in memory, deal automation, workflow management, and collaborative intelligence in the months ahead" (Rogo-1p, full page).
- **Pricing — rejection of the incumbent model:** the entire incumbent category (Rogo, Hebbia, AlphaSense) prices per-seat on seven-figure enterprise contracts, "which the market openly criticizes as a poor fit for the boutique and mid-market firms that are our wedge." Rejecting seats is both true to the thesis (price against labor value, not logins or tokens) and a wedge; seat pricing "punishes our own thesis: if we let a firm run a deal with fewer junior bodies, seat pricing earns us less for delivering more" (Founders §9).
- **Pricing — recommended structure:** two-layer hybrid, no seats: (1) platform access fee tiered by deal capacity, ~70–80% of revenue (illustrative: small boutique up to 10 deals $150K–$250K/yr; mid-market up to 25 deals $400K–$750K/yr; larger advisory unlimited $1M+/yr — placeholders to validate with design partners); (2) deal-linked component ~20–30% — a fixed ~$10K deal-activation fee per mandate stood up (open with this) or a flat capped per-close fee, never a percentage of the bank's fee. Small-boutique tier deliberately undercuts incumbents' seven-figure minimums (Founders §9.1, §9.3; Deck slide 15).
- **Pricing — rejected alternatives + legal flag:** pure success fee rejected (lumpy, long-dated, contestable attribution, and "taking a contingent slice of a securities-transaction fee can look like sharing transaction-based compensation, which draws FINRA/SEC scrutiny for an unregistered vendor. We want deal linkage, not deal participation"); pure usage/tokens rejected (caps value at cost, unpredictable spend, metering arguments at renewal); per-seat off the table per the founders. Non-optional FLAG: securities counsel must confirm any close-linked fee structure before it is offered to a firm (Founders §9.2, §9.4).
- **North Star metric — Net Labor Value Captured (NLVC):** Agreed Standard Task Value − AI/Platform Cost − System-Measured Human Operating Cost. STVs (agreed hours × loaded hourly cost by role) locked at pilot onboarding to avoid baseline debates; human effort measured passively (time-in-editor, version turns, redline volume), never self-reported. "The core pricing, renewal, and ROI metric"; the platform "prices against the labor value it creates, not against tokens consumed" (Blueprint §7 'The economics', §16, Appendix C; Deck slide 15).
- **Revenue projection / market framing:** path to $380M ARR in five years — ~6 banks at ~$0.8M ACV in Y1 scaling to ~120 banks at ~$3.2M ACV by Y5 (management projections, illustrative). "Vertical AI competes for the share of GDP spent on labor, not the sliver spent on software" — the cost compressed is the ~$91B labor share of the ~$137.6B fee pool (Deck slide 17; Founders §6.1 slide 11).
- **Long-term vision beyond banking:** "One platform. Many Opportunities" — expansion sector by sector into accounting, consulting, wealth, legal, insurance, tax; the ~$6.4T global professional-services market is the full opportunity, with the ~$137.6B IB fee pool as the first wedge (~2% of TAM). Close: "Software is still eating the world. It just reached services" — from products (appetizer) to services (main course) (Deck slides 18–19; Founders §2).
- **Network-effects growth model:** "Every deal seeds more customers" — a single sell-side deal puts Conductia in front of the client, its lawyers/accountants/advisors, every counterparty and their bankers/lawyers/accountants, each a pre-qualified prospect who sees the platform in action. "Our revenue is also our sales engine." Storm: "the sales model is also the platform or the platform is also the sales model"; buy-side is "the exact same process in reverse," so the product will eventually add buy-side process management linking to the sell-side (Deck slide 16; RTS-notes transcript).
- **Ignatious — founding design partner:** Storm's own tech M&A advisory firm; "Conductia is being built and pressure-tested on real, live sell-side mandates, not in a lab." Traction triad: Ignatious (live deals), Finalis (broker-dealer + compliance network for independent bankers — a regulated distribution path to many small/mid-market firms), Qatalyst-caliber network validation. "We are not starting from zero. We are starting from inside" (Deck slide 6).
- **Data sovereignty as strategy:** tenant isolation absolute by default — no client data leaves the firm's tenant, models stay stateless, no cross-client training; the firm always owns its data and derived intelligence; "the platform never monetizes one client's confidential information for another." Cross-firm benefit is strictly opt-in, de-identified aggregated telemetry only, compliance-reviewable and revocable (Blueprint §14 sovereignty box; see Process section for the Jul 2 project-vault direction).
- **Institutional memory as value prop:** Storm — a bank's millions of documents are "somewhat worthless to them" because knowledge dies when a banker leaves ("all that stuff still on the drive but basically who knows where it is… it just dies when I leave"); "one of our unique value propositions for banks is we create permanent institutional knowledge," with enterprise search a big aspect. The 2-pager frames the audit log dual-purpose: "regulator's record today, institutional memory tomorrow." Sibling value prop — cost attribution: every token traced to user × client × deal × prompt × model, answering the CFO who "wakes up… I just spent $100,000 yesterday on tokens and don't know how or why" (Storm reviews Ignatious token spend every morning) (RTS-notes transcript; Vision2p 'AUDIT LOG' + 'WHY IT WINS').
- **Why it wins — six pillars:** "We don't sell AI. We sell success" — senior leverage; end-to-end orchestration (one command center for tasks, drafts, diligence, approvals, closing); secure AI adoption; execution risk down (nothing missed or lost); quality & consistency (institutional best practice embedded in every deal, not trapped in heads); knowledge compounding without exposing client data. Tagline: "Banking in the front. AI in the back. Control everywhere" (Deck slides 1, 13).
- **Two build tracks must ship together:** "The Command Center without applications is plumbing with nothing flowing through it; a buyer cannot see value in a control tower that controls nothing. Applications without the Command Center are ungoverned point tools, which is exactly what a bank cannot adopt." First release must show both a governed operating layer and a focused set of applications/panels doing real trusted deal work. Compliance is "not a tax on the applications; it is what makes them sellable" — an app routed through the Command Center inherits supervision, permissioning, logging, and human gating for free (Founders §4 + §4.6).
- **Hold-the-line filter** (latest, 5-point test for every new idea): (1) runs ON the rails, never around them; (2) verdicts stay deterministic, external sends stay human-gated; (3) serves the sell-side wedge and the Ignatious launch — live by Labor Day, "magical" by mid-October; (4) automates low-judgment process, not decks, models, or valuations; (5) enters v1 only with a named owner and sign-off — "the same discipline the product itself enforces" (Vision2p 'HOLD THE LINE').
- **Pre-mortem strategic risks** (what the product must NOT become): building too broadly before nailing one workflow; "becoming an analyst tool instead of an operating platform" (mitigation: prioritize routing, policy, approvals, recordkeeping, auditability); "mirage product-market fit — revenue on human labor, not AI leverage" (mitigation: track AI-leverage and NLVC from day one, count human-in-the-loop labor as cost of delivery); model providers commoditizing/absorbing the category (mitigation: own the workflow graph, permissions, process data, feedback loops, compliance-native layer; keep models swappable); ROI measurement fights (mitigation: lock STVs at onboarding) (Blueprint §17).
- **Design-partner GTM motion:** the moat thesis "rests on bank access and clearing the security review that kills most banking startups." Offer: structured pilot on one live deal (parallel run), free or heavily discounted, in exchange for access, feedback, and a reference, run by forward-deployed ex-bankers — but on a discounted version of the real pricing structure so the pilot tests the real model. Plan for a 3–9 month vendor-risk/InfoSec review at any serious firm; win by starting it day one with the packet ready (SOC 2 path, pen-test plan, data-boundary maps, stateless-execution attestations, per FINRA 21-29). Investor milestones: design-partner LOI before/during tranche 1; working system on one live deal by Labor Day; 3–5 design partners in pilot within 18 months; NLVC evidence (Founders §8.1, §8 security box, §3.3, §9.4).
- **Raise framing:** $20M seed over 6–9 months, 18-month runway; use of funds: product & compliance build, design-partner success, go-to-market; milestones: paying banks on live deals, SOC 2, first marketplace partners. Staged tranches with a $25M–$50M post target for tranche 1; investor targets include Boldstart, Canapi, BTV, Afore; conflict screening excludes any investor backing Rogo or a direct competitor (Founders §10; Deck slide 19).
- **Regulatory honesty guardrails (don't overstate):** (1) FINRA 2210's hard pre-approval applies to RETAIL communications; most M&A counterparties are institutional, which gets relief from pre-approval (not blanket exemption) — the "MD approves every send" gate is firm policy + 3110 supervision, a safe default, not a 2210 requirement; don't tell a banker otherwise. (2) SEC 17a-4 no longer requires WORM — since the Oct 2022 amendment an audit-trail alternative suffices, so the append-only AuditEvent can itself be the compliance mechanism. (3) Notice 24-09 = no new rules but no grace period — supervision, recordkeeping, and 2210 attach to AI from day one, which is why the invariant exists (Handbook §2 'The rulebook').

## Process & workflow facts

- **Canonical lifecycle — five phases:** (1) Origination, Qualification, Mandate; (2) Preparation and Market Readiness; (3) Marketing and First-Round Bids; (4) Diligence, LOI, Definitive Docs; (5) Signing, Closing, Post-Close. Each has defined core activities, primary outputs, and an AI/human allocation (agent drafts/checks/summarizes/compiles; human owns pursuit decisions, conflicts, EL, narrative approval, outreach control, terms, closing items) (Blueprint §2 table).
- **Phase 1 includes pre-mandate ("pre-deal"/gate) work:** lead registration, source capture, conflict check, presearch, decision-maker mapping, pitch where required (explicitly optional in routing), valuation framing, buyer hypothesis, engagement letter. Outputs: presearch report, MNDA, preliminary diligence request, pitch deck, valuation pages, buyer hypothesis, mandate probability, EL (Blueprint §2 row 1 + §12).
- **Phase state machine in code:** enum `origination | preparation | marketing | diligence | closing`; `Phase.state = not-started | active | gated | complete | skipped` — each phase ends at a gate before the next begins; "skipped" is a first-class state. Deal is the single root entity; mandate/project/transaction are display aliases, never separate tables (Handbook §03 + §01).
- **DISAGREEMENT — phase granularity:** the seed deck presents only three phases (01 Preparation; 02 Outreach & Negotiation; 03 Confirmatory Diligence & Signing), omitting origination/mandate and post-close — a coarser investor framing vs the 5-phase blueprint/handbook model and Storm's ~82-step model (Deck slide 3 vs Blueprint §2 / Handbook §01 / RTS-notes).
- **The 82-step model:** Storm frames the sell-side process as ~82 steps "from a client calling to closing the deal," each step an "application" (his term) that can be human, agent, hybrid, or most likely multiple agents. McDonald's analogy: componentize the services process, rip the order slip into pieces, allocate each piece to a specialist (robot or human), reassemble at the end ("it assembles at the end to a closing dinner where the deal gets closed"). Firm-to-firm variation is mostly step-skipping, not different processes — configurable step-skip is real but not MVP-critical. RTS builds the first 16 skills; the planned embedded applications engineer takes the remaining ~70 (RTS-notes summary + transcript; Vision2p 'THE MENTAL MODEL').
- **Real-world deal duration:** a well-run sell-side process runs 6–12 months engagement-to-close (often 9–12): prep ~8–12 weeks, buyer outreach + first-round IOIs ~4–6 weeks, management meetings and second-round LOIs with 30–60-day exclusivity, confirmatory diligence to signing ~8–12 weeks; complex deals stretch 18+ months. Build implication: an agent touching deal artifacts holds MNPI for months, so retention, access logging, and wall controls must persist for the whole lifecycle, not per session (Handbook §01).
- **Step-skip rule:** any phase or step may be skipped or resequenced, but only with the firm-defined approver's logged sign-off (default approver: MD); routing is firm-configurable; code names apply to seller and every counterparty throughout (Blueprint §2 callout + §12). Storm's elaboration (flagged as his own view, not yet discussed with Lance/Roberto): existing relationship → skip pitch; NDA in place → "skip these five parts"; he'd like an agent that watches usage and auto-removes never-used steps ("this is not MVP by the way") or forward-deployed engineers configuring skips per firm; even a skip button is "not a make or break" for MVP (RTS-notes transcript).
- **Task anatomy:** every task carries execution mode (Human | Agent | Both), owner, reviewer, approver, due date, dependencies, risk tier, evidence. Handbook schema adds `riskTier: reversible | sensitive | irreversible` and `standardValue` (STV, locked at onboarding) per task; task state, `dependsOn[]`, and `evidence -> (Artifact|AuditEvent)[]` are first-class (Blueprint §8 + §9 journey 3; Handbook §03 Task schema).
- **Default approval gate:** for any external send or material release, reviewer = VP, approver = MD, firm-configurable via Policy. The nine roles are one enum on a single HumanActor — "who can approve this?" is a deterministic check, never a software judgment; seniority = authority to release to the outside world (Handbook §01).
- **Human sign-off triggers:** explicit human sign-off required to advance phases, send external materials, change access, or complete irreversible actions; §12 lists the gate triggers: any external send, material release, document execution, access change, or other irreversible/regulated action. Reversible work can be automated with logs; irreversible/regulated actions always require human approval on the record (Blueprint §10 + §12 + §8).
- **Source-of-truth precedence** (firm-configurable): executed/signed documents > the system of record for a given fact (CRM for relationships, VDR for diligence materials, model of record for numbers, approved deliverable for external claims) > latest approved version > logged sourced data > unsourced assertion. Unresolvable conflicts flag to the deal owner with both sources shown; "banker memory never silently overrides a logged, sourced record." In the handbook this is `Artifact.authority` — a precedence rank resolved by a deterministic function, never an agent adjudicating (Blueprint §11.1; Handbook §03).
- **Versioned outputs (pervasive):** latest approved version outranks earlier/unapproved drafts; Artifact rows carry version, authority, source, sensitivity, permissionTags, approvedState; prompt/output logging must include version tracking (FINRA 2026 report requirement); the Deliverables Tracker ('Later'-tier app) tracks teaser, CIM, model, updates, IOI/LOI analysis, and closing checklist by version, source, and approval; human effort is measured passively via version turns, redline volume, time-in-editor — no self-reported time (Blueprint §11.1, §14, §16, §17, Appendix A; Handbook Artifact schema).
- **Pre-Send QC — the checks:** every external item screened before release for recipient, attachment, code name, figures, claims, metadata, and required approval. KPI: 100% of covered external sends pass QC before release; blocked-error categories: wrong recipient, wrong attachment, hidden metadata, stale data, unsupported claim, inconsistent numbers, wrong code name, missing approval, confidentiality issue, permission breach (Blueprint §9 journey 5 + Appendix C).
- **Pre-Send QC — deterministic, no LLM in the verdict:** the QC screen is plain, testable code. Handbook worked example 3: an unsourced figure ("42% YoY growth" with no Artifact.authority match) BLOCKS the send like a failing referential-integrity check; the fix is a human sourcing the figure to the model of record and a deterministic re-run — never the agent retrying or the screen being relaxed; the block→source→pass→approve chain is preserved for regulators. The screen seed exists at `lib/compliance/screen.ts` and is "the crown jewel" (Handbook worked example 3 + §05 + testing strategy).
- **DISAGREEMENT — QC verdict shape:** Vision2p (v1.1, post-Storm, Jul 3) evolves QC to a green/yellow/red score that re-scores live as the banker edits — yellow sends with a logged acknowledgment; red blocks until green; agents cross-check each other's drafts before anything reaches a human. The handbook's worked example has a binary BLOCK with "no human override offered at this layer" — the yellow-with-acknowledgment path is new (Vision2p 'How one piece of work flows' step 4 vs Handbook worked example 3 step 2).
- **Canonical work-item flow (6 steps):** 1 Capture (code — ingest against deal, code-name participants, preserve copy) → 2 Route (code — orchestrator spawns task with mode/reviewer/approver; "it routes work; it never answers it") → 3 Draft (agent — budgeted killable run, cited from authoritative source, sealed envelope, cannot send) → 4 Check (code — Pre-Send QC) → 5 Approve (human — VP reviews, MD signs) → 6 Release + remember (code sends; full chain inbound→prompts→model calls→check→approval lands in the append-only audit log) (Vision2p 'How one piece of work flows'; Handbook worked example 2).
- **Day-in-the-life trace (blueprint):** buyer's corp-dev email auto-forwarded to the Command Center → system identifies the deal, checks the diligence tracker, flags that one answer depends on a missing data-room file (assigns owner + due date) → drafts reply in firm voice from model of record + approved CIM, marks one claim lacking backup so it cannot go out unverified → routes to deal VP with MD copy, pre-send QC on recipient/attachment/code name/figures/metadata → on approval sends, logs with sources, updates buyer log, tracks diligence task to closure. Result: no step skipped, near-immediate response, nothing unsupervised, complete record (Blueprint §5).

- **Sealed envelope + Job contract:** agents never touch raw firm data — only a permission-filtered, code-name-redacted context envelope, returning a proposal through the screen. Every non-human task dispatches as a typed Job declaring up front: sealed context, allowedTools/forbiddenTools/scopeCredentials, modelPolicy, budget (maxSteps/maxTokens/maxCost), stopConditions, reviewer, approver, riskTier, expectedOutputs, evidenceRequirements. Executing a Job produces a Run emitting ModelCalls + AuditEvents. Declaring ceilings before dispatch is what makes an agent "supervised by construction" (an uncapped agent = unsupervised actor = FINRA 3110 violation) (Handbook §03).

- **Regulatory anchor set:** FINRA Rule 3110 (supervision + WSPs — the product IS a supervision system), FINRA Rule 2210 (communications approval/review/recordkeeping), SEC Rule 17a-4 (record preservation), FINRA Notice 24-09 (all rules apply to GenAI, no grace period), FINRA 2026 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report (first AI-agent guidance: GenAI-tailored WSPs, prompt/output logging with version tracking, narrow scope, permissions, audit trails, human checkpoints before execution), FINRA 21-29 (vendor due diligence — Conductia is itself a vendor). - **2210 precision nuance:** hard pre-approval applies only to retail communications; institutional gets relief from pre-approval (2210(b)(1)(D)) but not from supervision/recordkeeping — the MD gate is firm policy + 3110, not a 2210 mandate. Trap: a comm can't be treated as institutional if it may be forwarded to a retail investor (live risk when a teaser reaches an individual principal) (Handbook §02).
- **17a-4 WORM correction:** since the SEC's Oct 12 2022 amendment (Release 34-96034), 17a-4 is satisfied by EITHER WORM OR an "audit-trail alternative" (records preserved so an original can be recreated if modified/deleted, time-stamped, capturing who changed what) — the append-only AuditEvent event-sourcing model can itself be the compliance mechanism, likely no certified WORM media needed, but final design must be validated with compliance counsel. Retention: communications ≥3 years (17a-4(b)(4)); FINRA 4511 6-year default otherwise (Handbook §02).
- **Audit substrate requirements:** AuditEvent is append-only with UPDATE/DELETE made physically impossible at the database grant level (insert-only role + trigger guard); every entity emits AuditEvents; legal hold must freeze a deal's AuditEvent/ModelCall/Approval/Communication set from purge/expiry. ## Terminology

- **Command Center** — the governed operating layer itself; the universal product/customer-facing name. Earlier materials' "operating system / operating layer" mean the same thing; reach for operating-system language only when explaining architecture and scale to investors. One-line thesis usage: "AI Command Center" for banker-adjacent listeners, "AI-native operating system" for architecture/moat/scale conversations (Founders §4 Naming box + §6).
- **Horizontal** — the platform layer common to every step, and by extension the team that builds it: Futureproofing (Lance, Roberto + engineers) building the rails — workflow spine, orchestration, permissions, compliance spine. The source docs mostly say "rails"/"platform"; horizontal/vertical is the org framing of the same split (RTS-notes 'Division of Work'; Vision2p 'RAILS + SKILLS').
- **Vertical** — the banking-specific skills/applications layer that runs on top of the rails, and the team building it: RTS Labs (Markus, Svetozar) + Ignatious bankers, extracting real workflows and building skills/agents. Every vertical must follow the rails' rules — "you can't sit on top if you're not [compliant]" (RTS-notes summary + transcript; Vision2p p.1).
- **Hybrid / edge-case verticals** — vertical (skill-layer) components built by the horizontal core team rather than RTS: PA-1 Compliance Comms agent is built by the core team in V1 (capture + draft-clean over Pre-Send QC); PA-2 Pitch/Mandate and PA-3 CIM Creator get seams only (slot + job contract + sign-off gate) pending decision Q1. The pre-RTS founders' framing had the core team + consulting firm building the entire first app set (Handbook §4 + §6 Q1; Founders §4.3).
- **Rails** — the platform: deal-workflow spine, orchestration, permissions + information barriers, approval gates, audit log, pre-send checks, model router, time/token tracking, cost attribution. "Nothing runs outside the rails"; "the rails never change — only each step's mode does" (Vision2p 'RAILS + SKILLS' + 'How it's used'; RTS-notes summary).
- **Skills** — the work: each of the ~82 sell-side steps as an app that can be human, agent, or both (examples: win mandate = HUMAN, KYC screen = AGENT, buyer outreach = AGENT+HUMAN, diligence Q&A = AGENT+HUMAN). "Application" in Storm's usage = one step of the 82-step process; each step may be multiple agents (Vision2p p.1; RTS-notes transcript).
- **Gate** — an approval checkpoint requiring explicit human sign-off on the record: phases end at a gate before the next begins (`Phase.state` includes `gated`); gates trigger on any external send, material release, document execution, access change, phase advance, or other irreversible/regulated action. Default gate: reviewer = VP, approver = MD, firm-configurable via Policy (Handbook §01 + §03; Blueprint §10 + §12).
- **Pre-deal actions** — the Phase-1 pre-mandate work before a signed engagement: lead registration, source capture, conflict check, presearch, decision-maker mapping, pitch where required (optional in routing), valuation framing, buyer hypothesis, engagement letter (Blueprint §2 row 1 + §12).
- **Focus vs Full Array** — the two project views in Rob's build: "Full Array" (Rob's "full rail") = the whole sell-side process, all steps, in one view; "Focus" = a single step's view, with back-and-forth navigation between the two (Rob-demo 00:09:02–00:09:58).
- **Dil console** — not a verbatim term in these eight sources. The canonical near-terms are the **Deal Console** — the hero first-build surface, the deal's home view (Blueprint §9 + Appendix A; Handbook §04) — and the **Diligence Tracker**, a fast-follow app (Blueprint Appendix A). If "Dil console" appears in later material, map it to the Deal Console (or a diligence-focused view of it) and verify against the source using it.
- **Step-lock** — NOT present in these eight sources by name (absence verified by grep across all six text sources). Nearest canon concepts: the step-skip rule (any phase/step skipped or resequenced only with the firm-defined approver's logged sign-off, default MD), phase gates, and the sign-off discipline (nothing enters v1 without a named owner and sign-off). If "step-lock" is used downstream it comes from documents outside this set (e.g., Jul 7 call notes) (Blueprint §2 + §12; Vision2p 'HOLD THE LINE'; Rob-demo 00:35:24–00:36:29).
- **Versioned outputs** — the pervasive versioning regime: every Artifact carries version, authority, source, sensitivity, permissionTags, approvedState; the latest approved version outranks earlier/unapproved drafts in the precedence hierarchy; prompt/output logging includes version tracking (FINRA 2026 requirement); deliverables (teaser, CIM, model, updates, IOI/LOI analysis, closing checklist) are tracked by version, source, and approval; human effort is measured passively via version turns and redline volume (Blueprint §11.1, §14, §16, Appendix A; Handbook Artifact schema).
- **Mandate** — a signed, engaged deal; in code, mandate/project/transaction are display aliases of one Deal root, never separate tables (Handbook Glossary + §03).
- **Sealed envelope** — agents only ever receive permission-filtered, code-name-redacted context, never raw firm data; proposals return through the screen (Handbook §03; Vision2p 'The one rule').
- **Job contract** — the typed dispatch for every non-human task: sealed context, allowed/forbidden tools, scoped credentials, modelPolicy, budgets, stop conditions, reviewer/approver, riskTier, expected outputs, evidence requirements; a Job's execution is a Run emitting ModelCalls + AuditEvents (Handbook §03).
- **STV (Standard Task Value)** — agreed hours × loaded hourly cost by role, locked at pilot onboarding to avoid baseline debates; the per-task value input to NLVC (Blueprint §16 + Appendix C).
- **NLVC (Net Labor Value Captured)** — Agreed STV − AI/Platform Cost − System-Measured Human Operating Cost; the core pricing, renewal, and ROI metric (Blueprint §7 + Appendix C).
- **Expert panels** — standing review panels (10 at launch) applying real published practitioner frameworks as a define→execute→review quality layer; never fabricate quotes; review layer, not a core dependency in v1 (Founders §4.4; Blueprint §8).
- **AuditEvent** — the append-only audit record every entity emits; physically immutable at the DB grant level; the substrate for regulatory reconstruction, legal hold, and (dual-purpose) institutional memory (Handbook §0 + sprint-0; Vision2p 'AUDIT LOG').
- **Code names** — default pseudonyms for the seller and every counterparty; real-identity key held in a separately access-controlled key store (deal team, compliance, management only), every deref logged; enforced at deal creation so the real company name never appears in subjects, invites, or URLs (Blueprint §8 + §13; Handbook §03; Vision2p 'The email gate').
- **Model of record** — the deterministic source of authoritative numbers in the precedence hierarchy; unresolved for a V1 that builds no model and bans LLM math (open decision Q8; recommended default: an ingested, owner-marked-authoritative artifact + deterministic checks) (Blueprint §11.1; Handbook §6 Q8; Vision2p 'KEY TECHNICAL CHALLENGES').
