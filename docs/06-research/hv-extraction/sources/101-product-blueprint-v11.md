# 4. The Five Magical Experiences

The product earns adoption through a few experiences that feel like magic on day one. The first is deliberately simple.

1.  **The morning command queue (for the banker).** A banker opens the Command Center and instantly sees the day across every live deal: what is overdue (how long, why, and who owns it), what is due today (prioritized, each assigned to a human, agent, or pair), a pre-drafted output for each task ready to edit, approve, or reassign in one click, what is in progress with status and ETA, what is risky or awaiting approval, the status and probability of each deal, and the non-deal commitments that still carry deadlines (client touchpoints, team, firm, and compliance tasks). Every deal has a single owner who approves each step and can add, remove, reassign, or resequence tasks.

2.  **Inbox to operating plan (for the deal record).** The banker keeps working in email. The Command Center sits on the deal as a member of the team: every relevant email, meeting, call transcript, diligence request, buyer reply, calendar event, file, and draft is converted automatically into a live operating plan and a permanent, searchable record. Nothing is re-entered, and nothing falls through. The banker can send anything to the Command Center, even a draft email to the client, and have it checked before it leaves the firm, for compliance and reputation. The Command Center routes it to the right reviewer, cleans it up, and releases it only on approval.

3.  **The background economics (for the CFO and COO).** The Command Center optimizes for cost and quality, and tracks both human hours and AI consumption by deal, by person or agent, and by task. Work that demands perfection (models, board decks, legal documents) is run through multiple models that check each other; routine work is run on the cheapest model that clears the bar. Spend is visible and governed, not a surprise at renewal.

4.  **The supervisory window (for compliance).** Compliance sees every communication, external material, approval, AI output, data source, permission change, exception, and record-preservation status in one place, without chasing bankers. Supervision becomes a live view instead of a reconstruction after the fact.

5.  **The compounding firm (for the managing partner).** Every deal makes the firm smarter. Approved templates, buyer feedback, pass reasons, relationship paths, diligence issues, negotiated terms, senior-banker edits, and post-mortems are preserved and reused, without leaking confidential client data and without training any model on it.

# 5. A Day in the Life: one sequence makes the product more concrete

1.  An MD receives an email from a buyer's corporate-development lead with new questions on customer concentration, which is automatically forwarded to the Command Center.

2.  The system reads the questions, knows which deal it is addressing, checks the diligence tracker, and flags that one answer depends on a data-room file that is still missing, with an owner and a due date assigned.

3.  It drafts a reply in the firm's voice, pulling the supported figures from the model of record and the approved CIM, and marks one claim that lacks backup so it cannot go out unverified.

4.  The draft routes to the deal VP for review, with a copy to the MD. Pre-send QC checks recipient, attachment, code name, figures, and metadata before release.

5.  On approval, the drafted reply is sent back to the buyer’s corporate-development lead, is logged with its sources, the buyer log is updated with the interaction and the open item, and the diligence task is assigned and tracked to closure.

**The result:** no step was skipped, the response was nearly immediate, nothing left the firm unsupervised, and the record is complete. The banker spent minutes, not the afternoon, and the partner can see exactly what happened and why.

# 8. Core Principles

- **Workflow first, AI second:** the product begins with the structured workflow of a deal, not a blank chatbot. AI operates inside the process.

- **Owner and execution mode on every task:** each task is Human, Agent, or Both, with reviewer, approver, due date, dependencies, risk tier, and evidence.

- **Compliance by architecture:** supervision, communications review, recordkeeping, information barriers, approvals, and legal hold are built in, not bolted on.

- **Permission-aware everything:** every search, draft, model call, tool call, and output is constrained by role, deal, client, firm, sensitivity, and policy.

- **Reversible vs irreversible actions:** reversible work can be automated with logs; irreversible or regulated actions require human approval.

- **Model-agnostic and sovereignty-ready execution:** route to the right model or tool by sensitivity, cost, latency, and quality; support emerging interoperability standards; and support local or edge execution where data sovereignty, latency, or cost require it, so no single model vendor holds leverage over the firm.

- **Labor value is the unit of value:** the Command Center measures the dollar value of the human work it absorbs or accelerates per task and prices against that value, not against token cost.

- **Code names by default:** the seller and every counterparty receive code names; the real-identity key is restricted to the deal team, compliance, and management, and every access is logged.

- **Firm-configurable governance:** engagement-letter routing, approver chains, disclosure lists, retention terms, and staffing are set by each firm, with global best-practice defaults.

- **Expert panels as a quality layer:** significant tasks and high-stakes communications are reviewed against the real, published frameworks of world-class practitioners (define, execute, review). Panels apply real frameworks only and never fabricate quotes. In v1 they operate as a review and quality layer, not a core dependency.

- **Persistent institutional memory, stateless models:** the model never trains on, learns from, or retains client data between sessions; all durable knowledge lives in the Command Center as governed, permissioned, versioned, exportable records. Models are swappable engines; the firm's compounding intelligence is the asset, and the firm owns it.

- **Data rights are strategic:** market-data licensing, client-data rights, and correction rights are designed in from the start.

## 11.1 Source-of-Truth Hierarchy

When systems disagree, the Command Center resolves conflicts by a defined precedence rather than surfacing contradictions. Defaults are firm-configurable.

- **Executed documents and signed agreements** outrank everything else for terms and obligations.

- **The system of record for a given fact** outranks copies: CRM for relationships and contacts, the VDR for diligence materials, the model of record for numbers, the approved deliverable for external claims.

- **The latest approved version** outranks earlier or unapproved drafts.

- **Logged, sourced data** outranks unsourced assertion or recollection.

- **Conflicts that cannot be auto-resolved** are flagged to the deal owner with both sources shown. Banker memory never silently overrides a logged, sourced record; it is captured as input and reconciled.

# 14. FINRA/SEC and AI Safeguard Architecture

**Compliance is the unlock made architectural.** FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09 (June 2024) confirms that technology-neutral rules and the securities laws apply to GenAI and LLMs, whether built internally or supplied by third parties. The Command Center is built around the firm's WSPs, supervision, communications review, recordkeeping, vendor governance, and information barriers. The 2026 FINRA Annual Regulatory Oversight Report (December 2025) expands this with first-time guidance on AI agents: WSPs tailored to GenAI and agent use, prompt and output logging with version tracking, and agent-specific controls (narrow scope, permissions, audit trails of actions, and human checkpoints before execution). Conductia is built to meet these expectations natively, preserving agent reasoning traces and prompt and output histories as records and shipping with GenAI-tailored WSP templates that map agent use cases to the firm’s supervisory system.

| **Control Area** | **Design Requirement** |
|----|----|
| **Supervision** | Map workflows to firm WSPs; route supervisory approvals; evidence principal review; escalate exceptions; preserve approval records. |
| **Communications** | Capture emails, calls, and messages where permitted; block unauthorized channels; classify and route regulated and external materials for approval; preserve final copies. |
| **Recordkeeping** | Preserve records, communications, approvals, AI outputs, agent reasoning traces, prompt and output histories, search queries, tool logs, versions, and sources in a form compatible with the firm's retention architecture. |
| **Information Barriers** | Deal walls, restricted and watch lists, MNPI classification, clean-team flags, code names for the seller and buyers, access requests, wall-crossing logs, and unusual-access alerts. |

| **Model Governance** | Approved-models list, no-training terms, vendor risk review, output review, fallback and swappable models with no loss of institutional memory, and customer-configurable policy. |
| **Market-Data Rights** | Respect vendor usage and redistribution restrictions; track source and license constraints. |

**AI Runtime Control Layer.** Every agent runs inside a runtime control layer, the safeguard against rogue agents, uncontrolled tool use, infinite loops, and runaway token consumption.

| **Safeguard** | **Requirement** |
|----|----|
| **Step limits** | Maximum reasoning and action steps per run, with escalation when exceeded. |
| **Loop detection** | Detect repeated plans, repeated tool calls, unchanged outputs, and circular dependencies. |
| **Token and cost budgets** | Budget by deal, person, task, agent, model, and run; escalate or stop when exceeded. |
| **Tool permissions** | Each agent has explicit allowed tools, forbidden tools, and scope-bound credentials. |

| **Approval gates** | Human approval required for irreversible, regulated, external, financial, legal, or permission-changing actions. |

**Quality and accuracy controls.**

- Source every material factual claim used in external work product.

- Use deterministic engines for math, models, reconciliations, and calculations; do not rely on LLM arithmetic for final numbers.

- Run consistency checks across decks, models, trackers, and emails.

- Flag stale data, unsupported claims, inconsistent valuation metrics, wrong company names, hidden metadata, and attachment mismatches.

- Use multi-model, critic-agent, and expert-panel review for high-stakes valuation, legal, compliance, and negotiation work.

- Track human edits to improve firm-specific style, quality, and senior-banker preference learning.

# 15. Deployment: The First 30 Days (Implementation Path)

Deployment is measured in days, on one live deal. Vendor and InfoSec clearance runs in parallel: no bank clears a core system that touches MNPI in 30 days through vendor-risk and InfoSec review, so Conductia provides a vendor due-diligence packet on day one (SOC 2 report, penetration-test results, data-boundary maps, and stateless-execution attestations, aligned with FINRA Notice 21-29). The timeline below is the technical pilot, which begins once the firm’s data-access approvals are in place.

1.  **Connect the systems (days 1 to 5).** Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, CRM, cloud drive, VDR, and calendar and email, through prebuilt connectors, with permissions mirrored from the firm's directory.

2.  **Load the firm's standards (days 3 to 10).** Import templates, style guides, and approved precedents; configure WSPs, approval chains, code-name rules, and information barriers.

3.  **Agree the baselines (days 7 to 14).** Set Standard Task Values for the covered MVP workflows so ROI is measured against pre-agreed numbers, not after-the-fact debate (Appendix C).

4.  **Pilot on one live deal (days 10 to 30).** Run the seven must-work journeys on a single active mandate; capture comms, maintain the plan, gate every external send, and produce the weekly update from the live record.

5.  **Review and expand (day 30).** Review the KPI dashboard with the executive sponsor and sequence in fast-follow surfaces as the deal reaches those phases.

# 16. KPIs Tracked (Success Metrics)

The true measure of Conductia is not AI activity. It is Net Labor Value Captured: the measurable economic value created when bespoke advisory work is converted into repeatable, governed, software-scalable execution. The platform should prove that it reduces senior-banker revision depth, advances live deal processes without prompting, increases useful client touchpoints, prevents missed commitments, blocks critical external-send errors, protects information barriers, and reuses institutional memory.

**Net Labor Value Captured** = Agreed Standard Task Value - AI / Platform Cost - System-Measured Human Operating Cost

| **Component** | **Definition / Measurement** |
|----|----|
| Agreed Standard Task Value (STV) | During pilot onboarding, Conductia and the bank agree on fixed baseline values for covered MVP workflows. Each STV is calculated as agreed human hours normally required for that task, by role, multiplied by the firm’s loaded hourly cost for those roles. |
| AI / Platform Cost | Actual model, token, routing, retrieval, storage, tool-call, and third-party API costs attributable to the task. |
| System-Measured Human Operating Cost | Human time measured passively inside the platform, including time-in-editor, workflow touches, approval review, version turns, redline volume, and senior-banker revision activity. No manual time tracking or self-reported time entry is required. |
| Example STVs | Weekly client update, buyer log refresh, diligence FAQ response, meeting-note summary, pre-send QC package, board update draft, buyer feedback summary, and VDR change review. |

The executive sponsor tracks the headline KPIs; product, compliance, and customer success track deeper telemetry. Appendix C specifies all of them with formulas and pilot targets.

# 17. Pre-Mortem

|  |  |
|----|----|
| **Pre-Mortem Risk** | **Mitigation** |
| **Building too broadly before nailing one workflow** | Ship the Command Center plus a thin four-surface shell first; prove daily utility on live deals. |
| **Compliance or security not bank-grade** | Design around WSPs, permissions, audit logs, data sovereignty, legal hold, and regulator export from day one. |
| **Market-data or client-data misuse** | Source provenance, license tags, permissioning, data-rights review, and explicit consent workflows. |
| **Runaway agent cost or behavior** | Enforce the Agent Runtime Control Layer: budgets, caps, stop conditions, loop detection, tool restrictions, and kill switch. |
| **Becoming an analyst tool instead of an operating platform** | Prioritize the Command Center: routing, policy, approvals, recordkeeping, and auditability. |
| **Mirage product-market fit (revenue on human labor, not AI leverage)** | Track AI-leverage and Net Labor Value Captured from day one; productize repeatable workflows; count human-in-the-loop labor as cost of delivery. |
| **Model providers commoditize features or try to absorb the category** | Own the workflow graph, permissions, process data, feedback loops, and compliance-native layer; keep models swappable; support local or edge execution so no single vendor holds leverage. |
| **ROI stuck in measurement fights inside the bank** | Lock Standard Task Values at onboarding and measure human effort passively (version turns, redline volume, time in editor), not by self-reported time. |

# Appendix B. Reference Anchors

- **FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09 (June 2024):** rules and the securities laws apply to GenAI and LLMs, whether internally developed or third-party supplied.

- **FINRA 2026 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report (December 2025):** expands GenAI guidance and adds first-time guidance on AI agents (autonomous, action-taking systems): WSPs tailored to GenAI and agent use, prompt and output logging with version tracking, and agent-specific controls (narrow scope, permissions, audit trails of actions, and human checkpoints before execution). Guidance, not new rules.

- **FINRA Regulatory Notice 21-29:** supervisory obligations for outsourcing to third-party vendors; Conductia is itself a vendor and must support customer vendor due diligence.

- **FINRA Rule 3110:** supervisory systems and written supervisory procedures.

- **FINRA Rule 2210:** communications approval, review, and recordkeeping.

- **SEC Rule 17a-4:** broker-dealer record preservation.

- **Internal source materials:** Conductia platform and product-idea notes, with general investment banking best practices.
