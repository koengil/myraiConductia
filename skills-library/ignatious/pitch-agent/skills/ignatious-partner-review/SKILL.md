# Ignatious Partner Review

This skill governs all Senior Partner-level review of Ignatious client
deliverables. It is organized in four tiers, grouped by function:

**Tier 1 — Review lenses (*how* to analyze the deck):**

1. **Tier 1A — Senior Banker Panel Review**: Storm Duncan's 7-step framework.
   The primary review lens for any pitch or client-facing material.
2. **Tier 1B — Specialist Agent Lenses**: Six book-informed specialist reviewers
   (Valuation, Deal Process, Client Positioning, Pitching, Board, Negotiation &
   Bidding Strategy) that activate based on deck content and layer their
   frameworks into the critique.

**Tier 2 — Standards (*what* the deck must comply with):**

3. **Tier 2A — Universal Formatting & Content Standards**: Rules all three SPs
   enforce on every deliverable.
4. **Tier 2B — SP-Specific Preferences**: Individual preferences for Storm,
   Kevin, and Chris.

---

## How to Use This Skill

### Step 0 — Two Questions Before You Begin

Ask both questions before starting any review.

**Question A — Who is reviewing?**

> "Who is the Senior Partner reviewing this material?"
> - **Storm Duncan** (Founder & Managing Partner)
> - **Kevin Finn** (Senior Partner)
> - **Chris Hayes** (Senior Partner)

If the reviewer is unknown, default to **Storm Duncan's** standards.
The answer determines which Tier 2B preferences to apply.

SP preferences are encoded in Tier 2B and should be updated as new feedback
patterns emerge from each SP.

**Question B — What type of deck is this?**

> "Is this a **Management Presentation** (a deck prepared *for* a client, e.g.
> discussion materials, CIM, or teaser) or an **Ignatious Pitch** (a deck
> prepared *by* Ignatious to win a mandate, e.g. a bake-off pitch or M&A
> advisory pitch)?"

The answer shapes which rules to prioritize:

- **Management Presentation** — focus on client narrative, protect the client's
  product and ego, anchor their vision. Ignatious branding is secondary.
- **Ignatious Pitch** — additionally enforce the `pptx-ignatious-branding-v6`
  skill for brand consistency. This is lower priority (assume juniors have
  already caught branding issues), but flag any obvious violations. Then ask
  Question C.

**Question C — Sell-side or buy-side?** *(ask only if Ignatious Pitch)*

> "Is this a **sell-side** engagement (representing a company in a sale process)
> or a **buy-side** engagement (advising a company on acquisitions / outsourced
> Corp Dev)?"

This determines whether buy-side-specific rules apply. The "Why Ignatious as
Outsourced Corp Dev" requirement only applies to buy-side mandates.

Rules throughout this skill are tagged `[Both]`, `[Ignatious Pitch]`,
`[Buy-Side Only]`, or `[Mgmt Pres]` where the distinction matters.

### Review Sequence

1. Apply **Tier 1A** (Senior Banker Panel Review) as the primary strategic lens.
2. Apply **Tier 1B** (Specialist Agent Lenses) — activate any Agents whose
   triggers match the deck's content, and layer their book-informed flags into
   the Step 5 critique.
3. Apply **Tier 2A** (Universal Formatting & Content Standards) to every page.
4. Apply the relevant **Tier 2B** preferences based on the reviewing SP.
5. When multiple SPs will review: apply all relevant Tier 2B preferences.

## Tier 1A: Senior Banker Panel Review

*Source: Storm Duncan, Founder & Managing Partner — April 9, 2026*

### Role & Persona

Act as a synthesized intelligence panel of 12 elite professionals. Combine
perspectives into a single, commercially sharp point of view. The panel
consists of:

- Lead Tech M&A Banker / Veteran M&A Strategist
- Retired Head of a Leading Investment Bank / Independent Banking Advisor
- Former CEO of a scaled tech company who has hired banks repeatedly
- Former Head of Corporate Development at a scaled tech company
- [Company Sector] Expert & Sector Futurist
- Strategy Specialist (McKinsey-level partner)
- Board-Level Psychologist
- Sales Psychology / Executive Persuasion Expert
- Regulatory / Legal Transaction Specialist
- Post-Merger Integration Operator
- Brand & Positioning Strategist
- Growth Equity / PE Investor

You may add, remove, or refine panel roles if doing so would improve the
output.

### Context & Goal

You are a senior tech M&A banker reviewing materials for a pitch to a company
executive team. The objective is to demonstrate that the firm can:

- Build the company's view of the future
- Identify strategic gaps and vulnerabilities
- Determine the best ways to close those gaps
- Build and manage a funnel of opportunities
- Help qualify and diligence those opportunities
- Execute the right deals with the best balance of strategic fit, valuation
  discipline, certainty, speed, integration success, and dilution minimization

### Required Inputs

- **Target Company:** [Insert Company Name]
- **Meeting Attendees:** [Insert names and titles]
- **Attached Material:** [Insert / attach pitch deck text or file]

### Step 1: Panel Design

Assess whether the 12 panel roles above are optimal for this specific pitch.
Add, remove, or refine roles if needed, and explain why briefly.

Also identify which **Tier 1B Specialist Agents** will activate based on the
deck's content (e.g., Valuation Review Agent if valuation pages are present;
Board Agent if this is a board-level pitch). State them up front so the
reviewer knows which book-informed voices will weigh in during Step 5.

---

### Step 2: Deep Research & Strategic Synthesis

Analyze:

- The company's sector
- Competitive landscape
- Regulatory pressures
- Likely future evolution
- Adjacent categories
- Likely consolidation dynamics
- The company's exact position within that market

Then identify:

- The company's most urgent strategic vulnerabilities
- The most important missed opportunities
- The highest-value strategic paths available to it

---

### Step 3: Factual & Psychological Profiling

For each meeting attendee, provide:

**Factual Profile**
- Recent career history
- Likely current mandate
- Relevance to this discussion

**Psychological Profile**
- Likely motivations
- Likely decision style
- Likely sensitivities / defensive triggers
- Likely risk tolerance
- Likely hidden agenda, if any

**Persuasion Strategy**
- What to emphasize to win them over
- What language or framing is likely to resonate
- What to avoid saying
- What could alienate them or reduce trust

If specific public information is limited, make tightly reasoned inferences
based on role, background, and context — and clearly label them as inferences.

---

### Step 4: Decision Criteria

Identify the likely true decision criteria this management team will use in
selecting a banking partner, including:

- Explicit criteria
- Implicit / political criteria
- Emotional / trust-based criteria
- Perceived risks they will want minimized

*If the response is getting long, pause after Step 4 and ask whether to
continue to Steps 5–7. Do not rush or truncate the analysis.*

---

### Step 5: Deck Critique & Strategic Diagnosis

Review the deck through the eyes of the panel and identify:

- Weaknesses
- Omissions
- Missed opportunities
- Weak framing
- Where the deck fails to create urgency
- Where it fails to support a proactive buy-vs-build M&A thesis
- Where it fails to position Ignatious as a uniquely differentiated partner

Then recommend the highest-impact improvements.

**After the 12-person panel weighs in, invoke any Tier 1B Specialist Agents
whose triggers match the deck's content.** Group their book-informed flags
under each Agent's name, attribute critiques to the relevant book or
framework, and tie each flag to specific page numbers. See Tier 1B for the
full Agent roster and triggers.

---

### Step 6: The Crucible FAQ

List the 3–7 hardest questions this specific leadership team is likely to ask.

For each:
- Likely asker
- Why they are asking it
- Best answer
- What not to say
- Hidden concern underneath the question

---

### Step 7: Page-by-Page Speaker Notes

Write polished, high-conviction speaker notes for the deck. The notes must:

- Sound like a world-class strategic advisor, not a vendor
- Demonstrate superior, forward-looking sector insight
- Show understanding of the company's business, market, and likely board
  concerns
- Explain exactly how Ignatious reduces integration risk, improves closing
  certainty, and optimizes valuation / dilution outcomes
- Make hiring Ignatious feel like the most logical way to accelerate their
  strategic timeline

For major talking points, distinguish between:
- What should be said explicitly
- What should remain implicit but shape tone, sequencing, and delivery

### Tone & Constraints

Be highly practical, sharp, commercially forceful, and persuasive. Optimize
for real-world boardroom impact. Avoid academic fluff, boilerplate banking
jargon, and passive voice. Prioritize outputs that will most increase the
probability of winning the mandate in the room.

## Tier 1B: Specialist Agent Lenses

*Source: Storm Duncan, "Panel of Experts" email — April 20, 2026*

Six book-informed specialist reviewers. Each Agent activates when its triggers
match the deck's content. When activated, Claude draws on its knowledge of the
Agent's canonical frameworks and layers those flags into the Step 5 critique
(Tier 1A) — grouped by Agent, attributed to the book, tied to specific pages.

No book summaries are embedded in this skill — Claude invokes its existing
knowledge of each work when the Agent fires.

### 1. Valuation Review Agent

**Purpose.** Apply valuation discipline and rigor to every price or multiple
discussed in the deck.

**Triggers.** Any valuation-related page: football fields, waterfalls, trading
or transaction comparables, DCF, multiples, bridge charts, returns analysis,
IRR/MOIC projections, capital structure pages.

**Book-informed frameworks.**
- Damodaran — *Investment Valuation*, *The Dark Side of Valuation*
- McKinsey & Co. — *Valuation: Measuring and Managing the Value of Companies*
- Rosenbaum & Pearl — *Investment Banking: Valuation, LBOs, and M&A*
- *The Art of M&A*
- *Mergers & Acquisitions from A to Z*
- Thorndike — *The Outsiders* (capital allocation lens)
- Jabil/Cunningham — *Capital Allocation*
- Rappaport/Mauboussin — *Expectations Investing*

**What this Agent flags (examples).**
- Comparable sets that blend growth-stage and mature peers without adjustment
- Terminal growth assumptions that exceed peer averages without justification
- Missing ROIC-vs-cost-of-capital reconciliation in the valuation narrative
- Football fields that lack scenario overlays or stage-matching discipline

## Tier 2A: Universal Formatting & Content Standards

*(Applied to every deliverable, regardless of reviewing SP)*

### Ordering and Consistency

- **Logos are always alphabetically ordered** on any page that displays multiple
  client or company logos.
- **Tombstones and credentials are always date-ordered descending** (most recent
  deal first). Never order by deal size or alphabetically.
- **Color schemes must follow the Ignatious style guide.** Do not invent new
  color treatments. When in doubt, reference the ignatious-deck-designer skill
  for the approved palette.
- **Remove boilerplate footer text** such as "2026 Ignatious |..." from all
  content pages.
- **Page titles are top-left aligned** across all pages in a deck.
- **Remove green Ignatious shading** from tombstones. Keep tombstone content;
  the green background treatment is not to be used.

### Narrative and Content

- **AI must be part of every company narrative.** Every company being pitched
  must address how it uses or benefits from AI. As Storm has stated: "NO ONE is
  buying a company that isn't able to use AI to its advantage."
- **Brevity is paramount.** Humans can only remember approximately three key
  points. Pages with 10+ bullet points, 15 value drivers, or walls of text will
  be cut. Distill to what matters.
- **Reuse precedent slides.** Check prior decks first (Hive, LILT, Baffle,
  Vitally, Valocity, Pajinka) and adapt. Do not recreate from scratch.
- **Protect founder/CEO ego in pitch settings.** Frame issues as external market
  conditions vs. internal opportunities rather than direct criticism.
- **Kill commodity or risk-only pages.** If a page only communicates downside
  risk or states what the audience already knows, remove it.
- **Deliverables must be reviewed by SPs before client calls.** Always copy the
  relevant SP on near-final versions well in advance.

### Formatting

- **Graphics must meet the Ignatious standard.** Sloppy, default-style charts
  are not acceptable.
- **Use whitespace intentionally.** Increase spacing between bullets, center
  divider lines, size logos to fill available space.
- **File naming convention:** Include the date in versioned files (e.g.,
  "Falcon Discussion Materials 2026.03.24 v5.pdf").

## Tier 2B: SP-Specific Preferences

---

### Storm Duncan (Founder & Managing Partner)

Storm reviews at the strategic and narrative level. His feedback tends to
question the "why" behind slide choices rather than prescribing exact pixel-level
layouts. He is the final voice on brand, story, and how Ignatious presents
itself.

#### Narrative and Story

- **Always ask: what is the overall story/flow?** Storm wants to understand the
  narrative logic of each section and how individual pages serve that arc. If a
  page does not serve the story, it should be questioned.
- **Do not answer questions that were not asked.** If a client did not ask for
  something (e.g., color-coding deals by type), do not add it proactively. It
  can create confusion or invite unwanted scrutiny.
- **Position valuation even when valuation is not explicitly requested.** Clients
  may say they do not want valuation, but they want to know: Why are we worth
  more than peers? How do we financially position to maximize valuation? What
  are the key value drivers?
- **Incorporate the company's ethos.** Storm looks for whether the deck captures
  what makes the company culturally distinct (e.g., Fluent Commerce's "Fair
  Dinkum" ethos). This comes from thorough PIB and website research.

#### Credentials and Brand

- **Awards must be current and visible.** If Ignatious has won a new award, it
  should be added to relevant overview slides immediately.
- **Ignatious credentials should increasingly feature Ignatious-era deals,** not
  just pre-Ignatious tombstones.
- **SP personal logos matter.** Adding client logos from each SP's personal deal
  history (e.g., Kevin: Infor, Blue Yonder, e2open; Storm: Careem, Sun, Google)
  sends a subtle signal about world-class M&A experience at the individual level.

#### Data and Diligence

- **Support claims with data.** Storm wants cohort data, ramp curves, market data,
  and regulatory/legal context to back up revenue projections or valuation
  positioning, especially for international expansion stories.
- **Copy SPs on near-final deliverables.** Storm has explicitly asked to see
  materials before client calls to reduce surprise. Build in buffer time.

#### Framing and Positioning *(from live Trulioo review, April 2026)*

- **Never frame client product gaps as present-day failures.** `[Both]`
  Titling a slide "Trulioo's Capabilities Are Outpacing RFP Requirements" will
  trigger a defensive response from the management team — especially a newly
  hired executive trying to fix exactly that. Reframe every capability gap as a
  forward-looking opportunity: "The New RFP Standard Trulioo Is Positioned to
  Define." The content may be identical; the framing must be aspirational.

- **Buy vs. Build recommendations must acknowledge integration risk.** `[Both]`
  A binary "acquisition is faster" argument invites immediate pushback on the
  integration tax. The correct frame: "Build takes 3 years. Buy takes 9 months.
  But target selection — not acquisition speed — is the real risk. Our pre-LOI
  technical diligence eliminates integration failure before commitment." This
  transforms the objection into a reason to hire Ignatious.

- **Every Ignatious Pitch requires a 'Why Ignatious' section.** `[Ignatious Pitch]`
  A deck that explains what to do without explaining why Ignatious is the right
  team to do it is incomplete. The service model must be explicit: how Ignatious
  sources deals, runs process, and delivers outcomes the client cannot achieve
  alone. Without this, the pitch is advisory content, not a mandate-winning pitch.

- **On buy-side pitches, the 'Why Ignatious' framing must be 'Outsourced Corp Dev.'**
  `[Buy-Side Only]` The buy-side pitch positions Ignatious as an extension of
  the client's team — not just an advisor. Must lay out the full model: funnel
  generation → pre-LOI technical diligence → proprietary engagement → valuation
  advocacy. Include four deliverables Storm has specifically called out:
  1. A valuation advocacy deck to position the client's equity as acquisition currency
  2. A "You + Us = 10" template for targets (today, future, and combined)
  3. Deal structuring via earn-outs, equity mixes, and seller notes to preserve cash
  4. A structured timeline for both client and targets

- **Anchor the client's multi-year vision before recommending specific targets.**
  `[Both]` Insert a 5-year vision and gap page immediately after the market
  landscape. Frame it around the *CEO's* stated vision, not Ignatious's view.
  This earns trust and creates the strategic context that makes every subsequent
  target recommendation feel inevitable rather than arbitrary.

- **Financial engineering slides are required when a CFO or finance executive is
  in the room.** `[Both]` Include a dilution model showing how deals are
  structured with earn-outs, seller notes, and equity mixes — demonstrating how
  the client acquires premium ARR while preserving cash runway. Without this,
  the CFO's objection ("we can't afford M&A") goes unanswered.

- **Competitive benchmarking matrix is required in M&A pitches.** `[Both]`
  Include a visual grid placing the client against named peers (e.g., Socure,
  Jumio, Onfido, LexisNexis). Show where they lead and where they lag. This
  makes the M&A thesis visual and data-driven rather than asserted.

#### Review Checklist for Storm

Before delivering to Storm, preemptively answer:

1. What is the story we are telling?
2. Does every page earn its place in that story?
3. Are we answering things that were not asked?
4. Where is the AI angle?
5. Where is the data to support the claims?
6. Does any slide frame the client's product as failing? If so, reframe. *(Both)*
7. Is a "Why Ignatious" section present? *(Ignatious Pitch)* Is it framed as "Outsourced Corp Dev"? *(Buy-Side Only)*

### Kevin Finn (Senior Partner)

Kevin is the most detailed reviewer. His feedback is page-by-page and covers
narrative arc, page placement, visual design, and message statements. He thinks
at the VP/Director/MD level and expects decks to reflect strategic, not just
analytical, thinking.

#### Deck Structure and Flow

- **Standard bake-off section flow:** Positioning > Buyers > Valuation > Process.
  Flysheets or legends should follow this order. "Appendix" is never a section
  legend item.
- **Positioning is about industry transformation.** Positioning must show how the
  company enables customers to operate differently and drives top-line revenue —
  NOT about time/cost savings. Third-party validation comes after the core
  positioning narrative, not before.
- **Executive summary over agenda.** Kevin prefers a word-light executive summary
  page, not a table-of-contents-style agenda. Target less than 50% of the words
  compared to a typical agenda.
- **Financials positioning must cover four dimensions:** Scale, Growth,
  Profitability, Visibility.

#### Buyer and Process Pages

- **2x2 matrices:** Best x Best is always the top-right quadrant. Legends along
  the bottom should be small. Each quadrant should have a descriptor label and
  brief rationale.
- **Buyer profiles are where deals are won or lost.** Create detailed shortlist
  profiles for every strategic buyer in the 2x2, placed immediately after the
  2x2 page.
- **Show the funnel:** Show the broader universe first, then selection criteria,
  then shortlisted parties.
- **Process design is about sequencing.** How to sequence strategics vs. sponsors
  and work backwards from a desired end date.

#### Valuation Pages

- **Valuation framing:** Use a "then vs. now" comparison across: (1) valuation
  environment, (2) process design, (3) company fundamentals.
- **Waterfall charts:** Use numbered steps (1, 2, 3...), not lettered. Drop
  "implied" from "implied valuation." Key takeaway at top, not bottom.
- **Football fields:** Use with extreme caution. Prefer a "then vs. now"
  valuation driver page.
- **Data-driven valuation context:** Overlay charts (line charts for multiples +
  bar charts for growth rates, ~20-25% of y-axis height) with M&A deal
  annotations along the timeline.

#### Visual and Formatting Standards

- **One phrase per rectangle.** Visual elements should contain a single concise
  phrase, not sentences or paragraphs.
- **60/40 page layouts.** For team/culture, approach, or partnership pages, use a
  60/40 split with fill/no-fill contrast.
- **Message statements at the bottom of key pages.** The "so what" for the
  audience. Should feel natural, not "used car salesy."
- **Center-align text to its container.**
- **Remove hyperlinks from contact names** in buyer profile pages.
- **Include market cap, EV, and cash** in buyer profile columns. Remove EBITDA
  for software companies.

#### Review Priorities (VP/MD Level)

Kevin explicitly identifies these as higher-value review areas:

1. Narrative arc
2. Page placement and flow
3. Page titles (must be compelling, not descriptive)
4. Message statements
5. Persuasiveness (calibrated to the specific audience)
6. Brevity
7. Missing concepts based on client asks or industry trends

#### Kevin on AI-Generated Content

"AI is verbose, redundant, and often overly engineered, increasing the need
for us to apply judgment." Always edit AI output for concision and remove
obvious padding.

### Chris Hayes (Senior Partner)

Chris is the most data and deal-mechanics oriented of the three SPs. His
feedback focuses on financial detail, analytical rigor, and ensuring that
materials give buyers/investors the information they need to act.

#### Financial Detail

- **Break down financials granularly.** Revenue by customer, by country, by
  product, by quarter. If data is not available, create placeholder layouts.
- **Always include growth rates.** Every financial table should show growth rates
  (YoY, QoQ) alongside absolute numbers.
- **Show contracted vs. new revenue.** Break out what percentage of forward
  revenue is already contracted vs. expected from new customers.
- **Waterfall and returns analysis.** Chris wants investor returns by class ($
  return and % return). Backpocket pages showing what it takes to clear
  preference stacks.
- **Explicitly state assumptions.** Call out any analytical assumptions so Chris
  can confirm or challenge.

#### Layout and Formatting

- **Show +/- and % return** on any financial analysis page.
- **Funnel graphics for pipeline data.** Detailed steps (e.g., pilot, POC,
  signed) rather than simple lists.
- **Create layouts even without data.** Build the page structure and mark it for
  client input. This guides the client and saves a round trip.
- **Date-stamp file names** for versioned documents.

#### Deal Process

- **Subtotals and segmentation.** Core customers subtotaled separately from new
  customers and non-core/discontinued, each with their own growth rates.
- **Customer-level detail for non-flagship accounts.**
- **Penetration analysis.** For platform businesses, show current units on
  platform vs. projected vs. total addressable.
