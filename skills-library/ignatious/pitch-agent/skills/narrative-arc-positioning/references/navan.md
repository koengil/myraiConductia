# Navan — IPO Roadshow, Oct 2025 (22 slides)

## 1. Company one-liner
"We make travel easy for every frequent traveler." The AI-native all-in-one T&E platform combining travel, expense, and corporate cards in one system.

## 2. Narrative arc
- pp. 1–3: **Hook + brand promise** — tagline + "Navan at a Glance" (ARR, customer count, spend managed, 4.1 avg-days-saved proof points).
- pp. 4–5: **Win-win-win value prop** — value framed for three stakeholders (traveler, company, finance). "End-to-End Platform" + "AI is a Cornerstone" — unified offering with AI as differentiator.
- p. 6: **"Amazon for Travel"** — AI-driven ecosystem framing where agents simplify end-to-end journeys.
- pp. 7–8: **Leadership + track record** — "AI-first since our founding"; consistent product advancement; growth momentum.
- pp. 9–10: **TAM + competitive landscape** — "$30B TAM" with dual-platform wedge; fragmentation-advantage positioning.
- p. 11: **Customer breadth** — diverse horizontal base (healthcare, ISVs, developers); sticky use cases.
- pp. 11–12: **Financial summary + GTM** — LTM revenue, gross margin, ARR growth; growth algorithm and strategic priorities.
- pp. 13–16: **Durability proofs** — land & expand; durable revenue growth; margin expansion via AI (62%→73%); H1 FY26 results.
- p. 17: **IPO benefits + recap** — R&D reinvestment; global platform; "one platform spanning the entire travel lifecycle."
- pp. 18–22: **Thank you + appendix** — pro forma balance sheet; NRR/GAAP reconciliation.

## 3. Positioning moves
- **T&E category reframing** — from fragmented tooling (separate expense, card, itinerary) to unified "one platform" for every traveler need.
- **AI-native (not bolt-on)** — "AI-first since founding"; AI as cornerstone driving 40–90% margin uplift and 30% time savings.
- **Horizontal ecosystem analogy** — "Amazon for Travel" claims platform breadth (air, hotel, car, restaurants) while staying rooted in the frequent-traveler wedge.
- **Dual-revenue moat** — corporate card (fintech) + expense management SaaS = reinforcing hooks, not isolated line items.
- **Narrow-wedge user focus** — "frequent traveler" as highest-value segment with quantified pain (7 min → 4.1 min for expense entry).
- **Implicit Concur/SAP contrast** — legacy T&E is bolt-on and fragmented; Navan is greenfield, unified, AI-native.

## 4. Opening hook (pp. 1–3)
Travel-lifestyle imagery + "We make travel easy for every frequent traveler." The glance slide immediately quantifies scale (ARR, 10K+ customers, spend managed, minutes saved). Frontloads **durability and size — not hype** — signaling public-company readiness.

## 5. Closing move (pp. 17–18)
"Expected Benefits of IPO" (reinvestment, global expansion, R&D, consolidation) + "Navan in Review" (reinvesting in R&D; broad global platform; mission-driven org; one-platform lifecycle). The "Thank you!" closer signals confidence; appendix grounds the story in institutional rigor (pro forma balance sheet, NRR/GAAP reconciliation).

## 6. Signature phrases
- "We make travel easy for every frequent traveler"
- "AI-first since our founding"
- "The 'Amazon' for Travel"
- "AI is a Cornerstone of Our Platform"
- "One platform spanning the entire travel lifecycle"

## 7. What's distinctive
Navan **rejects the point-solution-benchmark comparison** common in T&E pitches. Instead, it positions the unified platform itself as the moat — AI-native cost reduction, time savings, and embedded fintech are framed as reinforcing rather than isolated. "Amazon for Travel" claims horizontal breadth while the frequent-traveler wedge keeps focus tight. The deck frontloads durable metrics (NRR, margins, land-and-expand proof) over product feature counts. This is a **category-creator pitch, not a Concur alternative pitch.**
