# Chime — IPO Roadshow, June 2025 (33 slides)

## 1. Company one-liner
"To unite everyday people to unlock their financial progress." Chime positions as member-centric — not as a feature set or a disruption thesis, but as an engine for human outcomes for underserved Americans.

## 2. Narrative arc
- pp. 1–2: **Opener** — welcome + offering summary + mission.
- pp. 3–5: **Problem** — 95% fail at spending discipline, 67% have liquidity problems, 79% struggle to build credit; "traditional banks structurally cannot serve everyday Americans."
- p. 6: **Cost-advantage wedge** — "Our 3–5x cost advantage enables us to serve everyday Americans that traditional banks ignore."
- p. 7: **Product portfolio** — SpendBank, DraftPay, etc. Framed as low-cost innovation, not category creation.
- pp. 8–9: **Technology moat** — AI-powered optimization, transaction velocity, modern cloud infra — as efficiency driver, not tech-for-tech.
- pp. 10–12: **Habit & stickiness** — 87% check account 54x/month, 97% card use, 70% enable early paycheck; primary-account flywheel; 5–6x retention for multi-product members.
- pp. 13–15: **Brand equity** — "go-to brand for everyday Americans"; Time top-5 fintech; 80% community engagement.
- pp. 16–17: **Market leadership + team** — #1 direct-deposit-switch destination; exec talent from Netflix, Google, Uber, Apple.
- pp. 18–20: **Growth model** — 8.6M active members, $251 ARPU, 67% net retention; multi-lever recurring-payments model.
- pp. 21–22: **ARPU flywheel** — product adoption → ARPU expansion; cohort retention curves.
- pp. 23–24: **Execution proof** — 100%+ transaction profit retention, operating leverage.
- p. 25: **"Just getting started"** — six concrete adjacencies (savings, installment loans, digital cards, community, workplace, etc.).
- pp. 26–27: **Ecosystem / partnerships** — MyPay, bank partnership model, underwriting scale.
- pp. 28–33: **Closing + appendix** — Durbin Amendment framing, card transaction mechanics, non-GAAP reconciliations.

## 3. Positioning moves
- **Member-centric mission framing** — "unite everyday people to unlock their financial progress" precedes every product or metric claim.
- **Incumbent banks as villains** — structural indictment ("structurally cannot serve") backed with behavioral data, not tone.
- **Cost leadership as moral advantage** — 3–5x unit-cost edge framed as a member benefit, not a margin lever.
- **New category labeling** — "SpendBank" reframes a checking account into a named new category.
- **Habit-lock as moat** — daily engagement metrics (54x/month) carry more narrative weight than product features.
- **Brand equity as moat** — "go-to brand for everyday Americans" is positioned as independent of any single product.
- **Ecosystem partnerships, not disruption** — bank-partnership model reframes Chime from bank-killer to integrator (regulatory-friendly).

## 4. Opening hook (pp. 1–3)
Acknowledges regulatory scrutiny (disclaimer) then pivots immediately to a massive, underserved TAM, backed by hard behavioral data (95% failure at spending discipline). The frame isn't a moonshot — it's a scaled, 13-year operating business seeking capital to accelerate a proven model. This reads as mature and defensible to institutional investors.

## 5. Closing move (pp. 25–33)
Classic fintech IPO close: (1) inflection — "We're just getting started" with six concrete adjacent TAMs; (2) regulatory credibility (Durbin Amendment, transaction mechanics); (3) financial transparency (non-GAAP reconciliations). The six adjacencies act as the ask — each is a self-contained TAM (workplace alone reaches millions of employees).

## 6. Signature phrases
- "To unite everyday people to unlock their financial progress"
- "Traditional banks structurally cannot serve everyday Americans"
- "The go-to brand for everyday Americans"
- "Primary account relationships power our self-reinforcing flywheel"
- "We're just getting started"
- "3–5x cost advantage"

## 7. What's distinctive
Inverts the typical fintech IPO playbook by **leading with the human problem, not the technology**. Rather than opening with AI/ML or blockchain, Chime grounds its narrative in member behavior (95% spend-discipline failure, 87% check accounts 54x/month) and solves for that behavior. The villain isn't an abstract incumbent but a concrete structural failure. Cost advantage is framed as a moral prerequisite — not a margin accretion story but a feature of member service. Habit, engagement, and community carry more narrative weight than product features. The close lists six concrete adjacencies rather than claiming "category killer" status. It's a member-first, execution-driven narrative that treats the IPO as capital to extend proven dominance into adjacent segments.
