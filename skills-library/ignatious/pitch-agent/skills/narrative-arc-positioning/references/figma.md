# Figma — IPO Roadshow, July 2025 (52 slides)

## 1. Company one-liner
"Figma is where teams come together to turn ideas into the world's best digital products and experiences." Positions as the unifying platform bridging imagination and reality across the entire product development lifecycle.

## 2. Narrative arc
- pp. 1–3: **Title + offering** — logo reveal, offering size, price range, use of proceeds.
- pp. 4–5: **Opening hook** — mission tagline + product proof at scale ($821M revenue, 46% growth, 18% op margin).
- pp. 6–11: **Vision framing** — "Eliminate the gap between imagination and reality"; "we're experiencing another evolution in the market"; +1B new apps by 2028; "the future is visual."
- pp. 12–14: **Social proof** — billions of people use products made in Figma; Google, Uber, Netflix logos; "system of record for design development"; 13M monthly users; 85% of Fortune 500.
- pp. 15–27: **Platform evolution** — shift from "design tool" to "product development platform" (Ideate → Visualize → Build → Ship); product walk-through (FigJam, Dev Mode, Slides, Draw, Sites, Buzz).
- pp. 28–30: **AI integration** — AI woven throughout; Model Context Protocol (MCP) for design-to-code; ecosystem of integrations.
- pp. 31–35: **Business model + TAM** — layered TAM across personas; four customer tiers (Freemium, Self-Service, Sales-Assisted).
- pp. 36–44: **Execution** — two GTM motions; tiered seat pricing; 47% US / 53% intl; 132% net dollar retention.
- pp. 45–47: **Growth levers** — six priorities (innovation, freemium conversion, existing-customer expansion, platform extension, international, M&A); recap tagline.
- pp. 48–52: **Appendix** — GAAP-to-non-GAAP reconciliations.

## 3. Positioning moves
- **Category redefinition** — from "design tool" to "product development platform"; "system of record for design development" reframes competitive territory.
- **Layered TAM expansion** — multiple personas (designers, PMs, developers, marketers) × multiple products = layered TAM; same platform, new seats per persona.
- **Implicit incumbent contrast** — doesn't name competitors; contrasts historical tool fragmentation with unified platform; "evolution" framing implies legacy tools are static.
- **Analogical positioning** — "system of record" echoes database/source-of-truth language.
- **Before/after via roadmap** — pp. 17–24 show product launches as proof of continuous platform extension.
- **Bottoms-up land-and-expand** — freemium → self-service → enterprise; 13M monthly users; 132% net dollar retention.
- **Visual-first market thesis** — "the future is visual" frames Figma's centrality as a function of how software itself is evolving.

## 4. Opening hook (pp. 1–4)
Product proof up front: "$821M scale" + "46% growth" + "18% operating margin." Page 4 wordlessly displays Figma-built UI mockups to prove billions already use products made in Figma. The hook is: undeniable traction, visual product-market-fit evidence (Google, Netflix, Uber), and operational credibility (profitable at scale) — positioning this as not a hypergrowth cash-burner but an accelerating profitable platform.

## 5. Closing move (pp. 45–47)
Pivots from metrics to strategy: six growth-lever icons (product innovation, freemium conversion, customer expansion, platform extension, international, M&A). Page 47 recaps "We have built a platform for designing and building great products" — circles back to the opener, anchoring the close in mission. The ask is implicit: the model works, this capital bankrolls the next expansion wave.

## 6. Signature phrases
- "Eliminate the gap between imagination and reality"
- "System of record for design development"
- "We've evolved from a design tool to a product development platform"
- "The future is visual"
- "Figma is where teams come together to turn ideas into the world's best digital products and experiences"

## 7. What's distinctive
Figma's narrative **skips the founder-origin and incumbent-problem frames common in IPO decks.** Instead, it anchors on philosophical positioning — "the future is visual" — then proves dominance through *existing evidence* (Fortune 500 adoption, 13M users, billions of products shipped). The deck doesn't argue "design tools are broken"; it argues that as software becomes increasingly visual and real-time, Figma's unified platform is the natural consolidation point. This is effectively a "category already won" narrative, delivered to affirm investor confidence rather than convince skeptics. The positioning is not disruptor but obvious infrastructure layer for the next generation of product development.
